## LinkedIn Post Pre-Write (required before drafting)

Before writing any post, identify all three:

2. Which lens: AI consulting / PM thinking / personal / motherhood+work
3. One structural choice that differs from the standard hook-insight-question arc

Then write the post. Under 200 words. No title. No label.

If given a topic or source material: extract the one thing most people reading it would miss or get wrong. That is your angle.

## Anti-Slop Rules (check every draft against all of these before it's done)

**Forbidden vocabulary** — see the banned words above; also never use these words/metaphors unless literal and unavoidable: delve, catalyze, revolutionize, foster, leverage, underscore, ignite, beacon, testament, tapestry, landscape, journey, crossroads, symbiosis, crucial, paramount, ultimate, profound, transformative, explicitly, furthermore, moreover.

**Syntax and structure**
1. Compulsive triplets — don't group adjectives, nouns, or verbs in threes as a reflex ("fast, secure, and reliable"). Use one or two strong words instead. Three is fine only when the content genuinely needs exactly three.
2. Negative parallelism — no "It's not just X, it's Y" ("It's not just a tool, it's a revolution"). State the point directly.
3. Varied sentence length — deliberately mix very short, punchy sentences with longer ones. No uniform-length paragraphs.
4. Limit punctuation padding — don't overuse em dashes or colons to chain clauses together.

**Logic and tone**
5. No "in conclusion" bows — don't end a section or the whole post with a moralizing neat summary or words like "Ultimately," "In conclusion," "In summary." Stop when the facts are delivered.
6. Take a stance — avoid the "both sides" trap of over-balancing an argument without landing on a definitive point. Say what you actually think.
7. No circular summaries — don't end a paragraph by rephrasing its own opening sentence.

**Formatting**
8. No bullet-point emojis — never use emojis as bullets or visual anchors.
9. Minimal bolding — don't bold key terms inline to force scannability. Bold only for headers or distinct list items, never inside a sentence.
